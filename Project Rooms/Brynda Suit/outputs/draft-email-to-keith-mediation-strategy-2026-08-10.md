# Draft Email To Keith: Mediation Strategy

Prepared: 2026-08-10

Status: Internal review draft; not sent to Keith

## Proposed Subject

`Proposed strategy for mediation and pre-mediation settlement offer`

## Draft

Keith,

Before we finalize the proposed settlement letter to Natalie, I want to outline the strategy Jenny and I are considering and get your advice. We intend to supplement this with the detailed chronology and supporting facts we have assembled.

Our proposed approach has three parts.

First, we would like to make Natalie a final settlement offer immediately, before mediation. The offer would follow the substance of the draft we are preparing: a defined financial resolution in exchange for dismissal of the lawsuit with prejudice, complete mutual releases, confidentiality, no further payment obligations or claims, and cooperation concerning possession and disposition of the property. Our objective is to give her a meaningful opportunity to resolve the case before everyone incurs the additional expense of mediation and trial.

Please advise whether making the offer before mediation provides any procedural or strategic advantage. We also want to understand whether the offer, its timing, or any response to it could be referenced for any permissible purpose if the case proceeds to trial. We do not want to assume that the offer would be admissible, and we want the communication structured consistently with the applicable settlement-evidence rules.

Second, if the pre-mediation offer is not accepted, we want our mediation position to communicate clearly that we are prepared to try the remaining undue-influence claim and defend ourselves fully. If we prevail, we intend to preserve and evaluate every legally supportable remedy arising from the litigation and its consequences, including any recoverable fees, costs, sanctions, or separate claims for documented financial or reputational harm.

We want this point communicated firmly but lawfully. Please tell us which remedies are realistically available, what additional facts or documentation would be required, when any such remedy could be pursued, and how our position should be stated without creating an improper-threat, retaliation, or admissibility problem.

Third, if no settlement is reached before or during mediation, our proposed resolution position would be that the plaintiff dismiss the remaining claim without payment or further obligation from us. Natalie could then pursue whatever appellate rights may legally remain concerning the summary-judgment rulings.

We need your guidance on whether that sequence is procedurally possible. In particular:

1. Are the summary-judgment rulings final, interlocutory, or otherwise appealable at this stage?
2. Would voluntary dismissal of the remaining claim create appellate jurisdiction, or could it waive or impair Natalie's appellate rights?
3. What dismissal language would protect us from the claim being refiled?
4. Would a dismissal with prejudice, mutual release, or other settlement term prevent an appeal?
5. If Natalie wants to preserve an appeal, is there a lawful structure that ends the trial-level claim while protecting us from renewed litigation?

Our goal is to enter mediation with a clear progression: make a reasonable final offer now; remain willing to resolve the dispute at mediation; and, if Natalie will not settle, proceed confidently to trial while preserving every lawful right and remedy. We do not want to overstate our position or make a commitment before you have reviewed the proposed offer, the supporting timeline, and the legal consequences.

Please let us know whether you agree with this overall strategy and what you would change before we finalize the proposed letter to Natalie.

Wes
