# Revised Draft Email To Keith: Mediation Strategy

Prepared: 2026-08-13

Status: Internal review draft; not sent to Keith

## Proposed Subject

`Proposed strategy for mediation and pre-mediation settlement offer`

## Draft

Keith,

Before we finalize the proposed settlement letter to Natalie, I want to outline the strategy Jenny and I are considering and get your advice. We intend to supplement this with the detailed chronology and supporting documents we have assembled.

Our proposed approach has three parts.

First, we would like to make Natalie a final settlement offer immediately, before mediation. The offer would follow the substance of the draft we are preparing: a defined financial resolution in exchange for dismissal of the lawsuit with prejudice, complete mutual releases, confidentiality, no further payment obligations or claims, and cooperation concerning possession and disposition of the property. Our objective is to give her a meaningful opportunity to resolve the case before everyone incurs the additional expense of mediation and trial.

Please advise whether making the offer before mediation provides any procedural or strategic advantage. We also want to understand whether the offer, its timing, or any response to it could be referenced for any permissible purpose if the case proceeds to trial. We do not want to assume that the offer would be admissible, and we want the communication structured consistently with the applicable settlement-evidence rules.

Second, if the pre-mediation offer is not accepted, we want our mediation position to communicate clearly that we are prepared to try the remaining undue-influence claim and defend ourselves fully. If we prevail, we intend to preserve and evaluate every legally supportable remedy arising from the litigation and its consequences, including any recoverable fees, costs, sanctions, or separate claims for documented financial or reputational harm.

We believe the guardian-ad-litem record may create significant litigation exposure that should be evaluated before mediation. We have physical copies of the GAL petition, Brynda's will, and healthcare power of attorney. The petition describes Natalie as a disinterested party and appears to contain her notarized verification. The will predates the petition, gives the residue of Brynda's estate to Natalie outright, and names Natalie as personal representative. The record also shows that Natalie and her husband brought Brynda to Attorney Huston's office when the will was executed. Jenny further notes that the healthcare power of attorney gave Natalie a substantial medical fiduciary role before the petition was filed.

Those documents appear difficult to reconcile with the petition's disinterested-party representation. We want you to determine the legal significance after reviewing the exact documents, chronology, verification language, and filing circumstances. In particular, please advise:

1. Whether the petition or its use in the pleadings presents a viable Rule 11 issue based on factual grounding, reasonable inquiry, or improper purpose.
2. Whether a Rule 11 motion remains procedurally available after the GAL appointment was vacated, against whom it could properly be directed, and what notice or timing requirements apply.
3. What portion of our fees and expenses could potentially be recovered. We understand Rule 11 permits an appropriate sanction that may include reasonable expenses caused by the violating filing, not an automatic award of every defense fee.
4. Whether any other civil fee or sanctions mechanism, including the court's inherent authority or N.C. Gen. Stat. 6-21.5, should be evaluated.
5. Whether the verified statement, if proven knowingly and materially false, could affect the remaining claim, the credibility presentation, dismissal, or another remedy. We do not assume that vacatur itself established fraud or perjury.
6. Whether the evidence could support later civil claims such as abuse of process or malicious prosecution, what elements and favorable-termination requirements would apply, and when such claims could lawfully be asserted.
7. What responsibility, if any, opposing counsel could have under Rule 11 for signing or relying on the petition, and what evidence would be required before making that contention.

We want the civil exposure communicated firmly but lawfully as part of the settlement analysis. Please advise what can properly be stated to Natalie or her counsel about preserving sanctions, fee claims, and later civil remedies if the case does not settle. We do not want the settlement proposal to accuse anyone of a crime or professional misconduct without an adequate basis, and we do not want to use possible criminal reports or State Bar complaints as bargaining leverage.

Third, if no settlement is reached before or during mediation, our proposed resolution position would be that the plaintiff dismiss the remaining claim without payment or further obligation from us. Natalie could then pursue whatever appellate rights may legally remain concerning the summary-judgment rulings.

We need your guidance on whether that sequence is procedurally possible. In particular:

8. Are the summary-judgment rulings final, interlocutory, or otherwise appealable at this stage?
9. Would voluntary dismissal of the remaining claim create appellate jurisdiction, or could it waive or impair Natalie's appellate rights?
10. What dismissal language would protect us from the claim being refiled?
11. Would a dismissal with prejudice, mutual release, or other settlement term prevent an appeal?
12. If Natalie wants to preserve an appeal, is there a lawful structure that ends the trial-level claim while protecting us from renewed litigation?

Our goal is to enter mediation with a clear progression: make a reasonable final offer now; identify the documented civil exposure Natalie faces if the litigation continues; remain willing to resolve the dispute at mediation; and, if Natalie will not settle, proceed confidently to trial while preserving every lawful right and remedy. We do not want to overstate our position or make a commitment before you have reviewed the proposed offer, supporting documents, timeline, and legal consequences.

Please let us know whether you agree with this overall strategy, which sanctions or civil remedies are realistically available, and what you would change before we finalize the proposed letter to Natalie.

Wes
