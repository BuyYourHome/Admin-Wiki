## Revised Draft

Nikki,

I am writing to give you one final opportunity to resolve this case before mediation and before everyone incurs still more expense. I am being direct because this dispute could have been addressed without a lawsuit and can still be brought to a final end now.

Before filing suit, you could have contacted us about your concerns. We would have been willing to discuss a generous and practical resolution that protected Brynda's housing needs while avoiding the expense and damage litigation has caused. In a May 21, 2025 letter between counsel, our attorney stated that we remained willing to discuss a buyout of Brynda's lease in exchange for a full release and dismissal. I do not know whether that proposal was presented to you, so I am not suggesting that you personally rejected it. The point is that a negotiated resolution was available and remains possible.

We believe this lawsuit was unnecessary and that its allegations do not match what happened. Brynda contacted me while facing foreclosure. She said she had no one to help her and nowhere to go if the property was lost. The arrangement addressed both problems: it stopped the foreclosure and gave her the right to remain in the home indefinitely at an annual rent tied to the property-insurance cost. She accepted that arrangement and received its central benefit.

Brynda did not act without alternatives. Before contacting me, she met with another prospective buyer and took that buyer to Attorney Huston to discuss a proposed sale. She also testified that she was receiving many purchase inquiries because of the foreclosure and selected my communication because she considered it the most professional and preferred someone local. The exact amount of the other buyer's proposal is disputed in the record, but the important fact is not: she understood that investors wanted to purchase the property, consulted her own attorney, considered alternatives, and prioritized being able to remain in the home.

After the transaction, we treated Brynda with the same care and concern we would have shown our own mother. I drove her to medical appointments and provided a system for ordering groceries for delivery. Brynda herself testified that I was her emergency contact, was about the only person she saw outside county workers or the mail carrier, ordered groceries, took her to the bank and medical appointments, and responded when she needed help. We did not demand compensation for that assistance.

The foreclosure history raises a serious credibility issue. After Brynda met with the other prospective buyer and Attorney Huston, Huston contacted you and documented his understanding that you were willing to help Brynda financially. The record later shows that I cured the immediate arrears with a payment of $6,266.78. The opportunity to provide financial help existed before I became involved. Had that assistance been provided, the foreclosure potentially could have been cured without any transaction with me.

Your deposition testimony also presents issues that will have to be addressed if this case continues. You testified that you first learned about the foreclosure from Frances in 2024. That account is difficult to reconcile with Huston's September 2023 contact and contemporaneous email. Huston could not recall the exact words used during the call, so I am not overstating what he said; the timing and surrounding record nevertheless remain significant. You also admitted using Brynda's phone to communicate with me as though you were Brynda, allowing me to believe the messages came from her, and withholding your identity. That admission directly affects the credibility of the accusations now being made.

The guardian-ad-litem filing creates another serious issue. On September 30, 2024, about two weeks after our lunch, you and your husband brought Brynda to Huston's office, where she executed a will stating that she wanted property left to your daughters. That will was introduced as a confidential exhibit in Huston's deposition. Weeks later, you petitioned to become Brynda's guardian ad litem and represented yourself as disinterested, even though the requested return of the property could benefit your immediate family. The GAL appointment was later vacated after our counsel challenged the lack of notice, hearing, and an incompetency finding. Those facts do not by themselves establish wrongdoing, but they create credibility and procedural questions that will require explanation.

The case has now been narrowed substantially, with undue influence understood to be the remaining issue for trial, subject to the exact terms of the entered order. That claim is difficult to reconcile with the facts. I had known Brynda for only about two days and had approximately an hour and a half of conversation with her before the transaction. She contacted me, had consulted her own attorney about another proposal, was receiving other purchase inquiries, and had multiple options. I offered a solution that allowed her to avoid foreclosure and remain in her home; I did not control or overbear her decision.

Despite everything that has occurred, we are prepared to make one final settlement proposal before mediation:

After the property is renovated and sold, we will pay **100% of the positive net sale proceeds, if any**, calculated from the gross sale price after deducting the mortgage payoff, valid liens against this property, rehabilitation expenses, real-estate commission, closing costs, and documented taxes, insurance, utilities, and carrying costs through closing. The calculation will not produce a payment below zero. **[WES, JENNY, AND KEITH MUST APPROVE THIS FORMULA OR REPLACE IT BEFORE EXTERNAL USE.]**

In exchange, the settlement must include dismissal of the lawsuit with prejudice, complete mutual releases, confidentiality, no further payment obligations or claims, and full cooperation concerning possession, keys, remaining personal property, renovation, listing, and sale. Counsel must document the final terms in a signed settlement agreement.

This offer expires when mediation begins unless extended in writing. If it is not accepted, we will withdraw it and vigorously defend the remaining undue-influence claim through trial. If we prevail, we will preserve and evaluate every lawful remedy available for documented damages, attorney fees, costs, or sanctions, subject to our attorney's advice and the evidence. Continuing the litigation will add expense and uncertainty for everyone.

This proposal gives you the opportunity to receive the remaining positive net sale proceeds while bringing the dispute to a complete and final end. Accept the benefit being offered and end this lawsuit now.

Wes
