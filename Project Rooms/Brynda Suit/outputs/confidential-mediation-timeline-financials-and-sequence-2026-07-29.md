## Background

The defense account is that Wes gave Brynda the outcome she said she needed: the ability to remain in the townhome because she had nowhere else to go. The arrangement provided a lifetime lease at the cost of property insurance, described as substantially below market rent.

Wes did not meet Natalie until September 2024, approximately ten months after the acquisition and leaseback. By then the prior HELOC had been paid and the property had been refinanced, with proceeds reportedly used in the business on other properties. **(Wes & Jenny noted: they personally guaranteed the current mortgage.)**

The defense position is strengthened by Brynda's consideration of Wilson's competing purchase proposal. She took Wilson to her own attorney, understood that he wanted to purchase the property, and did not accept his proposal while prioritizing her ability to remain in the home. This is not a contradiction in Natalie's testimony, but it supports deliberation about both sale and continued possession. The Wilson offer amount remains disputed between the contemporaneous email and later testimony.

## Claims And Litigation History

Natalie and Noreen did not attempt to renegotiate the transaction with Wes before filing suit. The lawsuit asserted claims involving the disputed guardian-ad-litem procedure, fraud, Chapter 75, and punitive damages. Defendants also litigated the GAL appointment.

The defense raised potential Rule 11 concerns because claims allegedly relied on a power of attorney viewed as irrelevant to the sale. In plain terms, the concern is that the factual or legal basis for relying on that document may have been insufficient. This is a litigation position, not a finding that plaintiff or counsel violated Rule 11 or that sanctions will be imposed.

The presently verified lease-buyout response is dated May 21, 2025. It proposed discussing a lease buyout in exchange for a full release and dismissal without prejudice and rejected plaintiff's proposal that Wes purchase property his company already owned. The April 9 source and the alleged additional $281,000 demand still need to be located before their exact terms are used.

Discovery and summary-judgment proceedings followed, including a motion to compel and depositions of Brynda, Natalie, Frances, and Huston over two sessions.

## Rescission And The Remaining Alternative Claim

Rescission asks a court to unwind a transaction and attempt to restore the parties to their pre-transaction positions rather than simply award ordinary damages. The proposed summary-judgment order in the source set would leave only undue influence for trial, but it is not the signed, entered order. The operative order must be obtained before representing that rescission, Chapter 75, fraud, or other relief has definitively been removed.

Undue influence would contend, in plain terms, that Brynda's decision was improperly controlled or overborne by another person's influence. The defense emphasizes that Wes had known Brynda for only a few days, Brynda contacted him, she consulted her own attorney about another proposal, and she had multiple options. Those points support the defense view but are not a judicial determination.

Counsel should confirm what remedy remains available after Brynda's death, who has standing, whether plaintiff seeks an additional monetary judgment sufficient to address the mortgage, and the effect on appeal.

## Fraud, Chapter 75, And Appeal

**Fraud** *(in plain terms: an intentional material misrepresentation or concealment made to deceive, reasonable reliance by the other party, and resulting injury).* Whether those elements apply here is a legal question for counsel; the record should not be summarized as proving fraud by either side.

**Chapter 75** *(in plain terms: North Carolina's statutory prohibition on unfair or deceptive acts or practices in or affecting commerce).* If a claimant proves a covered violation and injury, assessed damages are trebled under G.S. 75-16. Attorney fees are discretionary and require the findings stated in G.S. 75-16.1; they are not automatic. See [N.C. Gen. Stat. Chapter 75](https://ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/ByChapter/Chapter_75.html).

The defense identifies a threshold pleading problem with the Chapter 75 claim, meaning the complaint may not allege what is legally required before the merits are reached. Counsel must identify the exact defect and whether the entered order adopted that position.

The defense assesses the likelihood of reversal and sanctions exposure favorably. Those are strategic assessments, not established outcomes. Any mediation statement should preserve rights without promising an unsupported countersuit or making a personal threat.

## Three Counsel-Review Credibility Issues

### 1. Foreclosure Knowledge

Natalie testified that she first learned of the foreclosure in summer 2024. Huston contacted her on or shortly after September 25, 2023, after meeting with Brynda and Wilson about the pending foreclosure; his contemporaneous email recorded his understanding that Natalie was willing to help Brynda financially. Huston could not recall whether he expressly used the word "foreclosure" or discussed the mortgage.

**Recommended wording:** Natalie's account is difficult to reconcile with Huston's contemporaneous record and testimony. The record does not conclusively prove what exact foreclosure information she received.

### 2. Guardian-Ad-Litem Position

Natalie's GAL petition represented that she was disinterested, while her daughters could benefit under Brynda's will if the property returned to Brynda's estate. The appointment was later vacated after a defense challenge concerning notice, hearing, and the lack of an incompetency finding.

**Recommended wording:** The disinterested-status assertion and procedure create a legitimate credibility and process issue for counsel. Vacatur is not, by itself, proof of dishonesty or improper purpose.

### 3. Text Impersonation

Natalie admitted that she possessed Brynda's phone, texted Wes in a manner that allowed him to believe the messages came from Brynda, did not disclose herself, and did so because she did not want Wes to know Brynda had left the home.

**Recommended wording:** This is an admitted deceptive communication practice directly relevant to credibility. Use the exact deposition admission rather than the broader unsupported label "lied three times."

### Historical HELOC And Cure Costs

Jenny's notes list a $28,267 HELOC payoff and a $6,267 reinstatement/cure. The record must establish whether the cure is included in the payoff.

- If the $6,267 is included in $28,267, subtracting the historical amount from estimated sale cash produces approximately **negative $10,959 to negative $20,959**.
- If the $6,267 is additional, the result is approximately **negative $17,226 to negative $27,226**.

The litigation materials use slightly different figures: $22,257.08 total purchase consideration, including $100 earnest money, plus a $6,266.78 cure payment, or $28,523.86 if additive. Counsel should reconcile the purchase agreement, payoff statement, cure receipt, and Jenny's figures.

### Avoiding Double Counting

The current mortgage payoff should be deducted from sale proceeds because it is a lien. If mortgage proceeds funded legal defense, the same legal spending should not also be deducted from that sale table without a tracing adjustment.

For mediation, use three ledgers:

1. **Property cash flow:** sale price, lien payoff, commission, rehab, and closing costs.
2. **Historical property investment:** acquisition consideration, HELOC payoff/cure, taxes, insurance, carrying costs, and improvements.
3. **Litigation expense:** legal fees, experts, lost time, and documented losses, adjusted to avoid counting mortgage-funded expenditures twice.

## Equity, Creditor Priority, Bankruptcy, And Collectability

The defense position is that legal expense has consumed the property's economic benefit and no settlement equity will remain after rehabilitation and sale. The current figures show only $7,308 to $17,308 of estimated sale cash before historical costs; they support that position only after the historical amounts are reconciled.

Collectability and bankruptcy are potential strategy considerations, not predetermined outcomes. A secured creditor generally looks first to its collateral, while an unsecured claimant may recover only from value remaining after valid secured claims and applicable costs.

## Proposed Mediation Sequence

1. Seek dismissal with prejudice, permanently ending the claims.
2. If dismissal is not achieved, address appeal risk and the cost of continued litigation.
3. Present the documentary chronology: foreclosure notice, independent-attorney consultation, competing proposal, Brynda's direct contact with Wes, signed documents, cure payment, and continued occupancy.
4. Use the foreclosure-knowledge inconsistency, GAL procedure, and admitted text impersonation precisely, with their limitations.
5. Present reconciled mortgage, cure, rehab, carrying-cost, and legal-expense records. Do not use "negative profit" until the HELOC figures are reconciled.
6. Preserve all rights to seek fees, sanctions, costs, or other relief that counsel determines the final record supports. Do not make a personal threat.
7. Explain that a pre-mediation agreement could reach the intended conclusion with less time and expense. Terms should address dismissal/release, payment or no further payment, confidentiality, possession and keys, personal property, and fees/costs.
8. Value any lease-buyout alternative separately. The May 21, 2025 response establishes a proposal to discuss a buyout, not its monetary value.
