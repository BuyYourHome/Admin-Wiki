I am writing to give you one final opportunity to resolve this case before mediation and before everyone incurs still more expense. This is intended to be direct because the time for avoiding the consequences of continued litigation is running short.

Before this lawsuit was filed, you could have contacted us directly about your concerns. We would have been willing to discuss a generous and practical resolution that protected Brynda's housing needs without the expense and damage this litigation has caused. Even after suit was filed, we proposed through counsel that Brynda's lease be bought out so she could receive the cash value of the housing benefit we had promised her. That proposal offered a way to resolve the dispute without continuing litigation, but it was not accepted.

We believe this lawsuit was unnecessary and lacks a sufficient factual basis. We did not exploit or mistreat Brynda. She contacted me while facing foreclosure, and we offered an arrangement designed around what she said she wanted and needed: a way to avoid foreclosure while remaining in her home indefinitely at a cost far below market rent. She accepted that arrangement and received its central benefit.

During the time Brynda remained in the home, we treated her with the same care and concern we would have shown our own mother. We took her to numerous medical appointments and devoted substantial personal time to helping with her day-to-day needs. We neither requested nor expected compensation for that assistance.

Brynda's handwritten journals are also inconsistent with the story now being advanced. They describe her hurt and resentment over your limited contact and visits for more than five years. We have found no written statement in those journals accusing me of mistreating, coercing, or exploiting her. The journals also describe her taking another investor to her own attorney to consider a genuine purchase proposal. Later evidence describes that proposal as approximately $140,000, although another contemporaneous record references $200,000. Regardless of the exact amount, she did not accept it because she wanted to remain in the home. Her later journal references to rent she owed further support that she understood she was occupying the property under a lease.

The foreclosure history raises another serious issue. After Brynda met with the other investor and Attorney Huston, Huston contacted you and documented his understanding that you were willing to help Brynda financially. You later testified that the immediate foreclosure problem could have been resolved for approximately $6,000. That opportunity existed before I became involved. Had the assistance Huston understood you were willing to provide actually been provided, the foreclosure potentially could have been cured without any transaction with me.

Your deposition testimony also presents substantial credibility problems. You testified that you first learned about the foreclosure from Frances in 2024. That account is difficult to reconcile with Huston's September 2023 contact and contemporaneous email. Huston could not recall the exact words used during the call, but the timing and surrounding record will have to be addressed. You also admitted that you used Brynda's phone to communicate with me as though you were Brynda, deliberately allowed me to believe the messages came from her, and withheld your identity. That admission directly affects the credibility of the accusations now being made.

The guardian-ad-litem filing raises additional procedural and credibility concerns. You represented yourself as disinterested even though members of your family potentially stood to benefit from the litigation's outcome. The appointment was pursued without the incompetency finding and procedure our counsel challenged, and it was later vacated. Those circumstances will also require explanation if this case continues.

The case has now been narrowed substantially, with undue influence understood to be the remaining issue for trial, subject to the exact terms of the entered order. That claim is difficult to reconcile with the facts. I had known Brynda for only about two days and had approximately an hour and a half of conversation with her before the transaction. She contacted me, had consulted her own attorney about another proposal, and had multiple options. The only influence I exercised was offering her a solution that allowed her to avoid foreclosure and remain in her home.

The final terms must be documented by counsel in a signed settlement agreement.

If we prevail, we will preserve and evaluate every lawful remedy available for documented financial and reputational damages, attorney fees, costs, or sanctions, subject to our attorney's advice and the evidence.
