## Photo Set Standard

Early photos may be used for identification, pricing, grouping, and a private preliminary approval packet. Mark them `preliminary`. Replace them with stronger later photos before publication when available, then recheck the listing if the later images reveal a different condition, included part, defect, or model detail.

Use the strongest available images in this order:

1. Clear full-item cover photo with a clean background.
2. Front, back, both sides, top, and bottom when useful.
3. Brand and model label, with personal or security-sensitive identifiers excluded.
4. Included accessories, cords, batteries, chargers, manuals, cases, or matching pieces.
5. Honest close-ups of wear, damage, missing pieces, rust, stains, cracks, repairs, or other defects.
6. A powered-on or working-state photo only when safely available and factually supported.
7. Scale or measurements for furniture, appliances, tools, and items where fit matters.

Do not improve photos in a way that hides defects or changes the apparent condition.

Permitted cleanup includes cropping, straightening, reasonable lighting and color correction, noise reduction, removal of ordinary background clutter, and a clean neutral background when the item remains accurate. Retain the original for comparison. Do not remove defects, change the item's color or shape, invent labels or accessories, or imply that an untested item works.

## Pricing Method

For every item or bundle:

1. Confirm the best-supported identity from visible brand, model, dimensions, and included parts.
2. Record current retail only as context, not as proof of used value.
3. Prefer recent sold or completed comparable sales when available; supplement with current local asking prices.
4. Adjust for condition, testing status, completeness, age, local demand, seasonality, transport difficulty, and time available before the estate sale.
5. Record three figures privately:
   - recommended asking price,
   - expected sale range,
   - quick-sale recommendation.
6. Mark confidence `high`, `medium`, or `low` and cite the comparison sources and access date.
7. Do not invent a price when identity or condition is unresolved. Record the missing fact first.

## Separate Listing Versus Bundle Rule

Prefer a separate listing when an item:

- has a recognizable brand/model buyers search for,
- serves a distinct buyer or use case,
- has meaningful standalone value,
- needs its own condition disclosure, measurements, or testing notes, or
- would be buried or undervalued inside a mixed lot.

Prefer a bundle when items:

- form one complete set,
- are matching or directly compatible accessories,
- are low-value separately but useful together,
- are duplicates or consumables likely to attract the same buyer, or
- cannot be described accurately without the rest of the group.

Avoid one broad "estate-sale items" listing when the inventory contains unrelated searchable products. Use thematic bundles only when they make the buyer decision easier and do not conceal condition differences.

Description rules:

- State included pieces, testing status, dimensions, and visible defects plainly.
- Use "untested" when that is the truth; do not imply working condition from appearance alone.
